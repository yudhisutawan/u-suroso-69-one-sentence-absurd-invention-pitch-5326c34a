the competition's own judge brief, criteria in order: (1) on-brief, (2) funny —
laugh from the invention itself, (3) clever fix — silly yet weirdly plausible,
(4) original — fresh absurdity, (5) tight — annoyance + invention + mechanism,
punch at the end. Margin 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelmingly better.

## Winner

**E (128 characters):**

> My printer now prints a formal excuse note for every page it refuses to print, signed in Comic Sans by its manager, the scanner.

Why it wins: the annoyance is real (printers refusing to print); the invention
is perverse in exactly the right way (a printer that does bureaucracy instead
of printing); the mechanism is three specific jokes stacked in one sentence —
a formal excuse note, signed in Comic Sans, by its manager, the scanner — and
every one of them comes from the invention itself, not bolted on. It beat the
two other finalists (G, I) head-to-head, both by a hair, and every other
candidate by 2+. No rebuild needed: a 9–0 undefeated winner that beat a strong
field is a clear winner under the playbook, not a known-weak entry.
